Enchanting

Drawing the power out of monster parts and binding it into a mundane item — grafted on, or transferred by a caster's will.

  1. A mundane item Manufactured or bought. You cannot enchant what does not exist yet.
  2. A monster component Set by the recipe. The component decides what the item becomes.
  3. A remnant The creature's essence or soul, gathered before it fades. This decides how strong the item is. Use a rarer remnant than the recipe asks and the item takes that higher rarity — along with its longer time and steeper DC.
  4. Enchanting check Rolled at the end of the enchanting time. Only a spellcaster can do it.
Enchanting check = 1d20 + spellcasting ability + skill for the creature's type

A wizard enchanting with a dragon's remnant rolls Intelligence (Survival) — the ability is the caster's, the skill is the corpse's.

On failure the enchantment can still take hold, but the item gains flaws in proportion to how badly the check missed. Miss by 13 or more and it is destroyed. Either way, the remnant and component are consumed.

Ancestral Weapons

One weapon per character, grown rather than replaced. Upgrades cost spirit points, earned through deeds rather than harvested.

A remnant may stand in for spirit points — but spend one that way and the weapon can never be enchanted again. At 20 points it awakens, takes 5 final points, and is finished.

Not yet automated. Socketing, rarity scaling, flaws and weapon progression arrive in Phase 5.
